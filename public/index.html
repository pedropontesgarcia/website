About

I am a graduate student in Computer Science at Cornell University working toward a Master of Science. I am part of the Capra research group under Adrian Sampson, where I research problems in systems, like hardware design, through the lens of domain-specific languages and compilers. I previously researched abstraction problems and profiling for the Calyx project, a compiler infrastructure for hardware accelerator generation. I am also a lead teaching assistant for CS 3410, Cornell's systems class. I have an interest in teaching and was nominated for for my college's annual TA award in 2025.

I am broadly interested in programming languages, compilers and systems. I enjoy topics with a theoretical flavor, such as language design and implementation, type systems, type theory, formal verification, and functional programming.

Outside of computer science, I teach for-credit outdoor courses with Cornell Outdoor Education, including cross-country skiing, backpacking, and canoe camping classes in the Adirondacks. I enjoy spending time in the outdoors, skiing, canoeing, and taking photographs. I am also interested in comparative literature and media studies, and came close to finishing a minor on it at Cornell.
